Project Overview

Mountaineering information about Paraná's Serra do Mar is scattered across OpenStreetMap, park management plans, community spreadsheets and old blogs. The atlas puts it on one map that loads on any phone, with no API key and no server.

The 2D map (Leaflet) is the default because it renders reliably on any graphics card; a 3D button loads Cesium only when asked, with real terrain and satellite imagery. Clicking a trail opens its elevation profile (maximum, minimum, ascent and descent) with a marker that follows the cursor on the map.

Each protected area carries its legal visiting rules under the SNUC law, and trails are marked official only where they match a trail recognised in the park plan; everything else is labelled unverified. A 13-chapter interactive story flies the camera through each massif, and visitors can drop their own GPX tracks onto the map.

A free public atlas that consolidates 273 named summits (validated against PeakVisor: 273 vs 275), 127 named trails, 462 points of interest and 440 climbing routes, with attribution and licensing for every source.

Methodology

  1. Summits, trails, points of interest, parks and cliffs fetched from OpenStreetMap (Overpass) and enriched with Wikidata.
  2. Trail elevation profiles from SRTM 30 m via OpenTopoData.
  3. Climbing routes compiled from a community spreadsheet: 322 routes and 118 boulders on 11 mountains.
  4. Visiting rules per protected area from the SNUC law and the park plans, with official and unverified trails kept apart.
  5. Everything embedded in one HTML file: Leaflet 2D, Cesium 3D on demand and a responsive bottom drawer on mobile.
  6. Optional Google Apps Script backend for community GPX uploads with moderation.
